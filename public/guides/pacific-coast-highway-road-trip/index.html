Roll the windows down, put on the Beach Boys and point the car south. Driving California's coast is the road trip from a hundred movies and a thousand family slideshows. It is the trip people take for honeymoons, milestone birthdays and "we've always talked about doing this" summers. It is still every bit as beautiful as the postcards your aunt sent home.

Check Big Sur road status first

Landslides have closed parts of Highway 1 through Big Sur for long periods in recent years. Before you go, check Caltrans QuickMap for current closures. When sections are closed, you can still reach parts of Big Sur from the north or the south and use Highway 101 inland to link the two halves.

How long to take

You could drive it in a long day, but three to five days lets you actually stop. Driving south keeps you on the ocean side of the road, with easier pull-offs for views.

Best stops, north to south

  • Santa Cruz, for the boardwalk and redwood parks nearby.
  • Monterey and Carmel, for the aquarium, 17-Mile Drive and Point Lobos State Natural Reserve.
  • Bixby Creek Bridge, the classic Big Sur photo stop.
  • McWay Falls in Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park, a waterfall that drops onto a beach.
  • Elephant seals at Piedras Blancas, near San Simeon, on view from a free roadside boardwalk.
  • Hearst Castle, the hilltop estate in San Simeon. Book tours ahead.
  • Morro Bay and San Luis Obispo, for a relaxed overnight.
  • Santa Barbara, for Spanish-style architecture, beaches and wine tasting.
  • Malibu, as you roll into Los Angeles.

Sample four-day plan

  1. San Francisco to Monterey and Carmel.
  2. Big Sur, as far as the road is open, overnight in Big Sur or Cambria.
  3. Elephant seals, Hearst Castle, Morro Bay, overnight in San Luis Obispo.
  4. Santa Barbara, then Malibu and Los Angeles.

When to go

Spring and fall usually have the clearest skies. Summer brings fog along the coast, especially in June. Winter storms can cause new slides and closures, so check road conditions daily.

Memory laneThen and now

The highway through Big Sur took 18 years to build and opened in 1937, connecting a stretch of coast that was once reachable only by horseback and boat. Bixby Creek Bridge, finished in 1932, has been on film and in photo albums ever since. Hearst Castle still looks just as it did when movie stars came up the hill for parties in the 1930s. Some things along this coast are new, like electric car chargers at the old filling stations, but the view at sunset has never needed an update.
