Winter in Arizona feels like a postcard from a gentler time: palm trees, cactus silhouettes at sunset and motel signs glowing in neon along the old highways. For generations of "snowbirds" from colder states, driving south to Arizona after the holidays has been a family tradition. This route follows in their tire tracks, from the desert to the red rocks to the edge of the Grand Canyon.

Sedona

Sedona's red sandstone formations are best explored on foot. Popular trails include Cathedral Rock, Devil's Bridge and Bell Rock. Trailhead parking fills early, and some areas use a free shuttle. Many trailheads require a Red Rock Pass or a federal recreation pass to park.

Grand Canyon in winter

The South Rim is open all year, and winter brings far fewer crowds and the chance to see snow on the canyon walls. Temperatures at the rim often drop below freezing at night, and trails can be icy, so bring traction devices if you plan to hike below the rim. The North Rim is closed in winter.

Tucson and Saguaro National Park

Saguaro National Park protects forests of giant saguaro cacti on both sides of Tucson. Drive the Cactus Forest Loop in the east district, or catch sunset at the Signal Hill area in the west. Tucson is also known for its Sonoran-style Mexican food.

Good to know

  • Desert nights are cold in winter even when days are warm.
  • Book Sedona lodging early for holiday weekends.
  • Most of Arizona does not observe daylight saving time.

Then and now

Historic Route 66 crosses northern Arizona, and towns like Williams, the gateway to the Grand Canyon, and Seligman still keep the Mother Road era alive with vintage diners, neon signs and classic cars. The Grand Canyon Railway first carried visitors from Williams to the South Rim in 1901, and after a long pause it was brought back to life in 1989. You can still arrive at the canyon the way travelers did more than a century ago. Add a stop in Williams on your way to the Grand Canyon.
