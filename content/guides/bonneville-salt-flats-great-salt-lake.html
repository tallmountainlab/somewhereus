There is a famous old photo from the Bonneville Salt Flats: a streamlined race car shooting across a white plain so flat and bright it looks like the edge of the world, with a crowd of men in hats squinting from the sidelines. Drive west from Salt Lake City today and you can stand in that same landscape. The road runs straight, the mountains float on the horizon, and then the ground turns white and goes on forever.

Northern Utah's salty west side is one of the strangest and most beautiful corners of the American West. This guide covers the Salt Flats and three classic stops around the Great Salt Lake.

Bonneville Salt Flats

The Salt Flats lie about two hours west of Salt Lake City along I-80, near the Nevada line at Wendover. They are what remains of ancient Lake Bonneville, a huge Ice Age lake that once covered much of western Utah. The salt crust is managed by the Bureau of Land Management.

  • The rest area: the Bonneville Salt Flats rest area on I-80 has a viewing area and information signs. It's a good first stop.
  • Bonneville Speedway: the access road off exit 4 near Wendover leads out onto the flats, where races are held in some summers.
  • The mirror effect: after winter and spring rain, a thin layer of water can cover the salt and reflect the sky perfectly. It makes for unforgettable photos.

Important: never drive onto wet or soft salt. Cars can sink and get stuck, and towing is very expensive. If the surface looks damp or dark, stay on the pavement. Bring sunglasses, because the glare is intense.

Around the Great Salt Lake

Antelope Island State Park

Reached by a long causeway about an hour north of the city, Antelope Island has a free-roaming bison herd, pronghorn, quiet beaches and some of the best sunsets in Utah. Bison have lived here since 1893, and the herd is rounded up once a year for health checks. Bring bug spray in late spring and early summer, when tiny biting gnats come out.

Spiral Jetty

The artist Robert Smithson built this 1,500-foot spiral of black basalt rock on the lake's north shore in 1970. Depending on the lake level, it may be underwater, on the shore, or surrounded by pink-tinted water and white salt. The last stretch is a long gravel road, so check conditions and bring plenty of water.

Golden Spike National Historical Park

On the way to the Spiral Jetty, stop at Promontory Summit, where the first transcontinental railroad was completed on May 10, 1869. Working replicas of the two original locomotives, Jupiter and No. 119, are displayed and run on many days in the warmer months.

Then and now

Racers first came to the Salt Flats in the early 1900s, and by the 1930s Bonneville was the place to chase world land speed records. British driver Sir Malcolm Campbell broke 300 miles per hour here in 1935, and later cars went far faster. Every August, Speed Week still draws hot rodders with homemade streamliners, though it has been cancelled in some years when rain left the salt too wet. Meanwhile, the salt crust itself has grown thinner over the decades, and people are working to protect it. Over in Wendover, the old army airfield where the crew of the Enola Gay trained during World War II is now a museum. Out here, history and landscape sit side by side, just as they did in those old black-and-white photos.

Suggested day trip

  1. Leave Salt Lake City early and drive west on I-80 to the Salt Flats (about 2 hours).
  2. Stop at the rest area, then drive out to the speedway area if the salt is dry.
  3. Lunch in Wendover, then head back east.
  4. Finish at Antelope Island for sunset (about 1 hour north of the city).

The Spiral Jetty and Golden Spike work best as a separate day, since they are in the opposite direction.

Good to know

  • Summer is hot and shadeless. Spring and fall are more comfortable.
  • Services are few between Salt Lake City and Wendover. Fill up with gas before you leave.
