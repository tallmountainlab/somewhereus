Walk up Park City's Main Street on a snowy evening and it feels like the front of an old Christmas card: brick storefronts glowing with warm light, snow piled on the wooden balconies, skiers clomping along in their boots and the neon sign of the Egyptian Theatre shining at the top of the hill. People who grew up in Utah remember coming up the canyon as kids in snowsuits, or watching the 2002 Winter Olympics on TV and seeing their own mountains on screen for the whole world.

Park City sits in the Wasatch Mountains, a short drive east of Salt Lake City. It is a ski town in winter and a mountain town in summer, with a real history underneath.

Winter: the ski areas

  • Park City Mountain: one of the largest ski resorts in the United States, with lifts right from town.
  • Deer Valley Resort: known for groomed runs and service. It has long been ski-only, with no snowboards allowed, so check its current policy.

Lift tickets are usually cheaper when bought online in advance. Both resorts host events through the season, and the season usually runs from roughly late November to April, depending on snow.

Historic Main Street

Main Street is the heart of town, lined with restored buildings from the mining days, galleries, restaurants and bars. Visit the Park City Museum in the old city hall to learn about the town's silver mining past, including a visit to the old territorial jail in the basement. The Egyptian Theatre, which opened in 1926, still hosts concerts and shows.

Utah Olympic Park

Built for the 2002 Winter Olympics, the Utah Olympic Park is still a working training center. You can watch athletes practice on the ski jumps, sometimes splashing into a pool in summer, and visit the free museums about Olympic history and skiing in Utah. Some seasons offer bobsled rides with a professional driver.

Summer in Park City

When the snow melts, the ski slopes turn into hiking and mountain biking trails. Summer days are warm and nights are cool. Look for the alpine coaster, the outdoor concerts and the Sunday farmers market. Many lifts run in summer for scenic rides.

Getting there and around

Park City is roughly 40 minutes by car from Salt Lake City International Airport via I-80, longer in snowstorms. Shuttles also run from the airport. In town, the free bus system connects Main Street, the resorts and many hotels, so you may not need a car once you arrive.

When to go

December to March is peak ski season, with the busiest times around Christmas, New Year and Presidents' Day weekend. January can bring the best powder. Late spring and fall are quieter and cheaper. Summer, from about June to September, is lovely for hiking, with wildflowers in the high meadows.

Then and now

Park City began as a silver mining camp in the late 1860s, and by the 1890s it was one of the richest mining towns in the West. In 1898, a great fire destroyed much of Main Street, and the town rebuilt the brick buildings you see today. As the mines declined in the twentieth century, Park City nearly became a ghost town. Then, in 1963, the Treasure Mountains ski area opened, and early skiers rode an old mine train, nicknamed the "Skier Subway," into a tunnel inside the mountain before a hoist lifted them up to the slopes. The town has been reinventing itself ever since, from the Sundance Film Festival, which made Main Street famous for decades, to the 2002 Olympics. The Winter Games are set to return to Utah in 2034, and Park City is expected to host events again.

Good to know

  • Altitude: the town sits around 7,000 feet. Drink water and take it easy on your first day.
  • Winter driving: carry chains or use a vehicle with good tires. Canyon roads can close briefly in storms.
  • Book early: lodging fills up fast on holiday weekends and during big events.
