Salt Lake City has a way of surprising people. Folks remember it from the 2002 Winter Olympics, when the whole world watched and the city glowed with snow and fireworks. Others remember family trips to float in the Great Salt Lake, which was impossible to sink in and left you crusted in salt. Come for a weekend and you will find both: a friendly, easygoing city with the mountains right at the end of the street.

Downtown

  • Temple Square, the historic heart of the city, with gardens and visitor centers.
  • The Natural History Museum of Utah, in the foothills, with dinosaur halls and great valley views.
  • Live sports: catch the Utah Jazz or the Utah Mammoth, the city's NHL team, at the Delta Center.
  • Food: try the restaurants in the 9th and 9th neighborhood and the Sugar House district.

Day trips

  • Antelope Island State Park on the Great Salt Lake, about an hour north, with bison, beaches and sunsets.
  • Big and Little Cottonwood Canyons, for hiking and wildflowers in summer and skiing at Alta, Snowbird, Brighton and Solitude in winter.
  • Park City, about 40 minutes east, with a historic Main Street, two big ski areas and the Utah Olympic Park.

Winter tips

Canyon roads to the ski resorts can have traction laws and long traffic lines on powder days. Check the Utah Department of Transportation for road conditions, leave early, and consider the ski buses. Rent a car with good tires or all-wheel drive if you plan to drive the canyons.

Good to know

  • The city sits at about 4,200 feet. Drink extra water.
  • Summers are hot and dry, while winters bring snow to the mountains and occasional inversions (hazy air) in the valley.
  • Southern Utah's national parks are about four hours south. Read our Mighty 5 road trip.

Then and now

Long before there was a ski lift, Saltair, a grand Moorish-style pavilion on the shore of the Great Salt Lake, drew crowds for dancing and swimming from the 1890s on. Generations of Utahns have stories about it. The lake has changed too, rising and falling over the decades. Little Cottonwood Canyon's Alta has been welcoming skiers since 1939, and it is still one of the few resorts that keeps the old-school, skiers-only tradition alive.
