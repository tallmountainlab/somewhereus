Think back to the family road trips of your childhood: the station wagon, the cooler full of sandwiches, a paper map that never folded back the right way and someone asking "Are we there yet?" from the back seat. Southern Utah is where that kind of trip still works best. The five national parks sit close enough to string together, and around every bend the red rock looks like the cover of an old View-Master reel.

The route

Most people fly into Las Vegas or Salt Lake City and drive a loop or a one-way route. Starting in Las Vegas, the order below keeps driving times reasonable.

Zion

Zion's main canyon is reached by the park shuttle for most of the year, when the scenic drive is closed to private cars. The two famous hikes are Angels Landing, which requires a permit issued by lottery on Recreation.gov, and The Narrows, where you walk up the Virgin River. Check flow levels and flash-flood warnings before entering The Narrows.

Bryce Canyon

Bryce is a natural amphitheater of orange hoodoo spires. Walk the rim at sunrise, then hike down among the hoodoos on the Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden trails. At around 8,000 to 9,000 feet, it is much cooler than Zion and can have snow from late fall to spring.

Capitol Reef

The quietest of the five. Drive the Scenic Drive, walk to Hickman Bridge, and stop in the historic Fruita district, where orchards are open for seasonal fruit picking and the Gifford Homestead sells pies. Scenic Byway 12, the drive in from Bryce, is a highlight on its own.

Arches

Arches has more than 2,000 natural stone arches. The hike to Delicate Arch is best in the late afternoon. In recent years the park has used a timed-entry reservation system during busy months, so check the official park site and book through Recreation.gov if one is in place.

Canyonlands

Island in the Sky, the district closest to Moab, has the easiest access and the big overlooks. Mesa Arch at sunrise is the classic photo. The Needles and The Maze districts are far more remote.

When to go

  • Spring and fall (roughly April to May and September to October) have the best hiking weather and the most visitors.
  • Summer is very hot in Zion, Arches and Canyonlands. Start hikes at dawn and carry plenty of water.
  • Winter is quiet and beautiful, with snow on the red rock, but some roads and services close.

Save on park fees

Visiting all five parks? An annual America the Beautiful pass usually costs less than paying each park's entrance fee. See our national park planning guide.

Then and now

In the 1920s, the Union Pacific railroad ran "Grand Circle" tours that took visitors from the train station to Zion and Bryce Canyon by touring bus, staying in rustic lodges along the way. Zion Lodge and Bryce Canyon Lodge still welcome guests today, and sitting on their porches feels like stepping into a sepia photograph. Many of the roads are the same ones families drove in their station wagons decades ago. Bring a paper map anyway. There is no cell service in a lot of these canyons, and it is part of the fun.
